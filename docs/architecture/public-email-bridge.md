# Public email bridge to Bindle

Status: provisional design, 2026-10-13. No receiving route, DNS record, sending identity, runtime feature or production capability is implemented or enabled by this document. Human reviewer: ______ Date: ______ Outcome: ______. Independent review: [public email bridge review](../release/public-email-bridge-review.md).

## Recommendation and boundaries

Offer inbound email at an owner's public `@3sln.me` handles as an explicit, off-by-default feature for currently paid accounts. It is a bridge into the owner's message resources and conversation invitations. Automated receipts remain one-way items; likely personal messages become pending conversation invitations. Ordinary email does not confer a Bindle identity, author signature, reciprocal admission or conversation history access. After verification and account setup, the sender can explicitly join and continue through the published conversation contract on their chosen provider.

Use the [conversation/contact design](conversation-contact-design.md), including independent owner copies, directed local peer approval, immutable email provenance, encrypted owner-bucket archives, portable resource references and the proposed `conversation/v2` contract. This proposal adds one bounded ingress adapter and narrow rule extensions, not an SMTP mailbox server, a new identity system or a second conversation protocol. It does not promise outbound personal email, arbitrary SMTP replies as signed participant events, IMAP or email-address portability between providers.

Unknown, disabled and ineligible recipients receive the same generic SMTP refusal, with a short service explanation or documentation URL where the transport permits. Do not accept those messages just to send advertising or an automatic explanation to a possibly forged sender. For an eligible, durably stored personal invitation, a strictly bounded, reply-safe acknowledgment can explain that the address uses Bindle and provide a signup/join continuation. A second notice when the owner accepts is allowed only after the intended sender independently confirms the bound mailbox, even if they have not completed account creation. Owner acceptance can wait safely for that confirmation. Suppression is preferable to an unsafe response.

## Owner settings and entitlement

Present one owner-only “Receive public email” setting, initially off, with the affected aliases, content-processing disclosure, limits, retention and reply behavior visible before enabling. Numbered and paid aliases resolve to the same stable owner UUID; eligibility attaches to the account, with an explicit allowed-alias set. Changing an alias must not strand stored data or confer access to the previous owner's items. Only currently assigned aliases route. Private associated email addresses are not public lookup aliases.

Settings include default destination collection, multi-label category handling, priority, notification channels, sender/address rules, a separate paid classification opt-in for this bridge, attachment policy, retention and whether safe transactional invitation notices are allowed. Contact-link classification is independently opt-in on each live parent grant; enabling the bridge never enables it on links or existing conversations. The shared [receipt/classification/alert policy](conversation-contact-design.md#receipt-admission-optional-classification-and-rejection-alerts) defines these separate controls. Owner-approved address rules are convenience filters, never cryptographic identity allowlists. A caller supplying an email address matching an approved sender cannot bypass content, quota or invitation gates. Public alias assignment/tombstones must prevent reassignment to another identity after account deletion as well as paid expiry; deletion must not let a new account collect a former owner's mail.

Effective enablement is the conjunction of owner preference, active account, current confirmed paid entitlement and provider availability. Entitlement expiry or downgrade disables new public-email intake while preserving the public alias, saved settings and previously accepted data under normal retention. A canceled subscription may remain eligible until its confirmed paid-through deadline. Provisional upgrades or stale refreshes must not extend service beyond known entitlement. Re-enabling after a lapse should be an explicit owner action in the initial version. Billing changes do not erase conversations or revoke unrelated Bindle contact links.

Recheck current enablement before initial durable admission; use a settings revision and bridge incarnation to resolve a simultaneous disable. Already committed items remain accepted and recoverable. Pending claims, replies, AI and notices recheck their bound live incarnation, relevant settings and budgets; disabling invalidates pending external claims and stops new bridge work, without erasing historical records or retracting an already submitted email. Re-enabling does not revive an old claim token. Show pause reasons only in the owner's account, not in SMTP responses that distinguish unknown, free, disabled or suspended identities. Paid expiry never recycles an owner's public alias to another account.

## Receive pipeline and truthful outcomes

1. **Admit the envelope.** Resolve only the runtime envelope recipient through the provider's existing directory/index. Never route by header `To`, `Cc`, `Delivered-To` or a body address. Reject unknown/disabled recipients, malformed envelopes, unsupported aliases and exhausted hard admission budgets before expensive parsing. Charge bounded ingress work even for rejected attempts; no attacker-controlled unique identifier may allocate an unbounded actor or persistent record.
2. **Reserve capacity and bound the stream.** Reserve recipient storage, processing and pending-work allowance. Check `rawSize`, then enforce limits while reading; a claimed size is not sufficient. Use a conservative initial application limit consistent with current adapters—provisionally 1 MiB raw and separately bounded decoded content—rather than advertising the platform maximum. Unsupported oversized messages are rejected as a whole, not silently truncated into a seemingly complete invitation.
3. **Parse once into inert content.** Apply the MIME limits below and extract immutable provenance and conservative automation hints. Reserve encrypted blob/checkpoint overhead as well as raw and normalized content if both are retained. An owner-authorized, provider-internal bridge admission is required; possession of a public handle is not a reusable contact capability.
4. **Commit before acknowledging.** Atomically record the local item/proposal, byte references, quota use, deduplication receipt, processing intent and eligible reply intent after required bytes are durably available. An SMTP handler success means this local acceptance, not recipient reading, sender verification or participant admission. Background `waitUntil` alone is not durable storage or a work queue.
5. **Classify and notify under durable work.** Run only reserved, bounded asynchronous work. Heuristics suffice when AI is disabled, unavailable or over budget. Keep undecided items quiet in a visible pending category until classification completes or a bounded timeout assigns an honest “unclassified” state. Never lose an accepted item because the classifier failed.

The optional initial reply is the exception to asynchronous processing: after durable commit, the still-live inbound event may claim and send its initial ordinal only when a conservative deterministic personal-message decision and all reply gates have completed within a small explicit event deadline. A bounded already-completed classifier may inform the decision, but intake must not wait indefinitely for AI. If classification remains pending or the event deadline passes, mark the initial ordinal suppressed; do not later send it through the general binding. Persisted intent records are evidence for deduplication and outcome tracking, not serialized `EmailMessage` objects. Catch reply failure separately from already committed intake so it does not falsely imply the stored message was refused.

Before acceptance, quota/format refusals use the transport's supported rejection. After acceptance, a processing failure becomes retryable local work or an owner-visible failure, not a fabricated SMTP rejection or a new bounce to an unverified address. The documented API exposes permanent rejection, not an arbitrary temporary-status setter. Establish actual transient failure/retry behavior with the configured provider before release; do not assume throwing produces a reliable 451. If storage durability cannot be established, never report successful acceptance.

Keep deduplication owner-scoped. `Message-ID` is attacker-chosen and is neither unique identity proof nor adequate deduplication alone. Specify a canonical ingress fingerprint from envelope context, normalized message identity/content and attachment digests, excluding provider-added trace variations; preserve original evidence separately. Test retries whose trace headers change, duplicate deliveries, missing IDs, deliberately reused IDs with changed content and simultaneous delivery to two aliases of the same owner. Different content under an established retry identity is a conflict, not permission to overwrite. Bound deduplication retention and disclose the resulting retry window; do not permanently suppress recurring legitimate mail merely because its content matches. With no reliable transport identity, distinguish the documented short-window duplicate heuristic from exactly-once delivery. Duplicate intake must not create another invitation, consume another response ordinal or extend an item's age timer.

## Content, provenance and conversation state

Choose a routing disposition of `receipt`, `personal-invitation`, `suspected-spam` or `unclassified`, with independent priority and notification policy. The optional paid classifier supplies multiple independent labels rather than this one-of routing choice: spam, conversation, outreach, promotion, transactional, receipt, automation, email verification and authentication code may overlap. Store versioned label IDs/scores, unknown state, model/policy/input provenance and owner corrections as recipient-private `classificationResult` metadata, separate from the existing single `classification` enum. Label probabilities need not sum to one; unknown or skipped inference is not evidence of safety. `Auto-Submitted`, list/report MIME types, `List-*`, bulk precedence, known one-time-code structure and no-reply patterns are conservative automation hints. Reply headers and conversational language are hints too. None is authoritative, and neither AI nor a sender-controlled header may auto-approve membership. Owners can correct classification without changing original evidence.

A receipt is an immutable one-way message resource, optionally grouped in a local owner-only thread. A personal email creates a pending conversation invitation containing **email-origin content**, not a fabricated Bindle participant event. Preserve asserted header author, envelope sender, relevant transport observations, provider receive time, normalized content digest and original attachment references. Render “Email sender not yet verified” where appropriate. A provider-signed ingestion record attests to receipt and processing, not to the purported sender having signed the content.

The invitation initially has an unresolved sender claim. Owner “Accept” records tentative willingness to exchange; it does not approve an unknown identity key, invent a guest account, hand an anonymous bearer the shared conversation secret or enable history. Once the sender verifies the bound mailbox, chooses a real account/provider, completes required setup, reviews the intended recipient and explicitly confirms, bind that proposal to their identity proof. Require owner review of the now-known identity/key, unless a previously explicit narrow owner policy already authorizes that exact verified binding. A differing identity/address requires renewed review. Use the conversation contract's proposal, acceptance, return-control allowance and independent local admission. First participant-authenticated content is a new signed event; a sender may explicitly adopt an identified email digest, but the original remains visibly email-origin and is never retroactively represented as originally signed. Any reply the owner drafts before mutual admission stays local and visibly unsent; signup/acceptance emails contain no draft body or historical transcript.

`In-Reply-To`/`References` may suggest grouping within this owner's already visible items. They cannot retrieve another owner's message, disclose a thread or select a canonical participant. Subsequent SMTP messages, including from an address previously verified once, do not satisfy independent per-event signature requirements. Initially they remain email-origin receipts/pending contributions with normal recipient policy; the normal way to reply as a participant is the Bindle app or another compatible provider. Full bidirectional email gateway authorship would be a separate explicit delegation design.

Keep all existing conversation privacy properties: owners hold independent copies; acceptance grants future exchange, not old history; block/erase/leave applies locally; edits and sender tombstones retain authorized previously received content; no body or key is given to an unapproved caller. Receipt import, local tagging and archival use existing message/resource contracts. Proposed email provenance and invitation-state schemas must be public, versioned contract extensions where interoperable, with declared capabilities and explicit unsupported errors. Do not add a private UI-only conversation admission route.

## Safe response and onboarding matrix

Automatic replies can create backscatter and loops. Follow RFC guidance by suppressing responses to null reverse paths, automatic submissions, delivery/status reports and list/bulk traffic; apply a durable sender/recipient cooldown and stamp outgoing automated messages accordingly. Do not copy untrusted body, headers or subject into generated replies. [RFC 3834](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3834.html)

For the initial version, a reply-safe candidate additionally requires an unambiguous parsed mailbox, strict agreement between envelope sender and single header `From`, no differing `Reply-To`, no no-reply/bounce/system pattern, no suppression entry, no prior bridge loop marker, and the platform's valid-DMARC reply gate. Restrictive matching will omit legitimate forwarded mail; the owner's inbox must explain that omission rather than silently choosing another target. These checks reduce risk but do not prove mailbox ownership or eliminate abuse from an authenticated domain. A separate finite global outbound budget remains mandatory.

Authentication results are trusted only when the adapter can identify results supplied within the receiving provider's documented trust boundary. A caller-provided `Authentication-Results: ... dmarc=pass` is not such evidence; neither its apparent position nor an arbitrary `authserv-id` suffices without an established sanitization/provenance rule. If reliable extraction is unavailable, do not display “authenticated” or weaken response policy. Built-in reply's own enforcement remains necessary. [RFC 8601](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8601.html)

| Situation | Owner outcome | Sender response / continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unknown, disabled, unpaid or suspended recipient | No message persistence beyond bounded abuse accounting | Same generic SMTP refusal. No fresh email. |
| Oversize, malformed, hard quota refusal | Not accepted; bounded reason/accounting | Supported SMTP refusal; no generated bounce. |
| Receipt, auth code, list, automated response, DSN or suspected spam | One-way receipt/quarantine under settings | No bridge signup email. |
| Personal invitation, unsafe or ambiguous return address | Pending, visibly unverified | No automatic reply; owner can retain/reclassify. |
| Personal invitation, all reply gates pass | Durable pending invitation | At most one immediate logical acknowledgment, subject to live budgets/cooldown and configured reply adapter. |
| Owner accepts; sender mailbox not confirmed | Acceptance waits without disclosing it to the asserted sender | No acceptance-specific email. |
| Owner accepts; mailbox confirmed, account setup unfinished | Pending confirmed continuation, still no participant admission | At most one additional transactional join notice, if sender consented to it and owner permits it. |
| Sender has an account here | Pending until authenticated account choice and proof | Sign in using normal credential flow; associate/verify the exact mailbox if needed; explicit final join. |
| Sender has an account elsewhere | Same pending proposal | Choose that provider; existing safe HTTPS handoff and resource/identity proof; do not force a new local account. |
| Sender has no account | No hidden active account | Verified contact, credential and tested recovery setup, then explicit recipient/account review and join. |
| Owner declines, blocks, expires or erases invitation | Terminal local state; cancel pending notices/work | Generic unavailable continuation; no rejection/reading/activity disclosure. |

Suggested immediate copy: “This address receives messages in Bindle. Your message was stored as a conversation invitation; the recipient has not accepted it yet. To continue, choose your Bindle account or set one up.” Use “stored” only after durable commit; never imply delivery into the recipient's attention. No advertising, original-message quotation, owner profile details, private email association or indication of presence/read state belongs in this acknowledgment.

The HTTPS continuation contains a short-lived random invitation reference, not the conversation shared secret, contact capability, app token or account session. Store only its digest server-side, bind it to the exact owner/proposal/eligible mailbox and limit its purpose. Initial GET is an inert preview, safe for mail scanners. It neither consumes verification nor exposes invitation content/acceptance. An explicit POST begins the mailbox challenge in the current browser, with CSRF protections, abuse limits and expiry. Prefer the existing independently delivered short code/session-bound verification mechanism; the invitation link alone is not proof of email control. Challenge messages initiated by the person are separate from the two unsolicited bridge response ordinals and remain subject to existing verification throttles.

Mailbox confirmation may exist as a short-lived, narrowly scoped continuation **before** account creation: it proves control for this proposal and permits the opted-in one-time acceptance notice, but creates no login, signing identity, general inbox access or membership. This satisfies the before-signup followup case without pretending that a verified address is already an account. If acceptance happened earlier, confirmation plus notice consent can enqueue that one followup; never send a retrospective notice automatically on an unrelated later login.

On signup, preserve existing mandatory verified reachable contact, credential creation and tested recovery. Reuse verification only where the identity engine can safely bind the exact verified mailbox, purpose, freshness and browser flow; otherwise run the normal setup challenge. For claiming this invitation, require fresh mailbox control bound to the proposal, bridge incarnation and selected identity key; a historically verified association here or an external provider's asserted `verified_email` alone is insufficient. After account/key choice, the selected identity signs the bridge's single-use claim nonce and explicit join intent, and the same browser presents the mailbox proof. A pre-account confirmation may authorize the minimal second notice, but cannot be replayed to bind a different key without a new key-bound confirmation step. A pending identity allocated during setup has no conversation authority. Existing associated-account conflicts must go through normal authenticated account choice and uniqueness rules; do not reveal associated account existence to unauthenticated callers or merge accounts. Email verification is not login unless the user deliberately enabled and invoked the normal email-login mechanism. Guessed message IDs and public email lookup cannot enumerate pending invitations.

Legitimate `Reply-To` differences deserve an explicit resolution screen. Show the original address roles as assertions, explain why no automatic target was chosen and verify the intended mailbox. A user cannot change an address-bound proposal to an arbitrary mailbox merely by possessing its URL; a differing claimant requires an owner-approved amendment and renewed identity review. The same applies when the envelope return path is an automated bounce address. Do not automatically associate every observed address with the sender's profile.

Use durable notice ordinals `initial` and `accepted`, tied to the same immutable proposal, plus sender/owner and provider-wide cooldowns. Before sending, atomically claim one ordinal and recheck enablement, sender confirmation where required, owner consent, terminal state and suppression. Provider send acceptance is not delivery. The current API cannot guarantee exactly-once external email after a lost response: keep an explicit `outcome-unknown` state and do not automatically resend uncertain unsolicited notices. A permanent bounce suppresses further notice attempts. No fallback through the general outbound transport may bypass built-in reply restrictions. Use a bounded claim lifetime and explicit failure outcome so concurrent workers cannot multiply sends.

## MIME, storage and privacy

Use a reviewed streaming MIME parser with limits on raw and decoded bytes, header length/count, nesting, part count, attachment count, filenames and parsing time. Reject ambiguous envelope/header syntax for automatic response; do not execute scripts, open links, fetch remote images, unpack arbitrary nested archives or run active attachment previews. Decode transfer encodings with bounded expansion and reject malformed encodings rather than accepting contradictory renderings. Preserve a safe text alternative and convert HTML through the existing inert rendering model described in [messaging](../spec/messaging.md). Content type and filename are untrusted labels. Attachments remain opaque downloads or separately approved inert previews; inline CID references resolve only to admitted local parts.

Separate raw-source retention from normalized presentation. If forensic raw MIME is kept, it is never rendered directly and counts fully against quota. Incoming HTML cannot initiate tracking callbacks; receipt links are explicit user actions. Auth codes and sensitive mail are excluded from lock-screen previews by default. Do not send auth codes, attachments, raw headers or entire histories to AI; text classification uses a minimized, bounded representation, with deterministic redaction of recognized secrets before inference. Classification opt-in must disclose cloud processing; redaction is best effort, not a guarantee that arbitrary sensitive content has been removed.

Archive accepted email content, attachments, provenance, invitation claim/notice state, rules, replay receipts and retention frontiers into the owner's reserved bucket namespace under the existing conversation/account checkpoint plan. Cold conversation segments are encrypted to that owner's assigned public encryption key; the separately held recovery file unlocks private keys for downloads and migration. Provider key custody means this is not end-to-end encryption against the host. Raw source and sensitive bridge metadata need the same archive envelope; keeping bodies encrypted while addresses/subjects remain in an unrestricted index is insufficient. Document the deliberately retained operational metadata and its access/retention separately.

Storage accounting includes content, duplicated raw/normalized bytes, attachments, encrypted envelopes, manifests, indices, pending-work state and retained generations. Essential revoke/erase bookkeeping has a bounded reserve; spam cannot consume an unlimited exception. Backup/export must include the declared consistent cut of accepted hot work and bucket checkpoints. Ordinary live bucket listing is not a complete atomic backup. External attachment references are not a backup of external bytes. Migration preserves email-origin history/settings and necessary encrypted work state, but provider-bound receiving aliases, verification challenges and outgoing sender configuration need reissue or reconnection; do not reactivate stale transport tokens at the new provider.

The public identity document and directory remain minimal keys/identity/protocol metadata. Email intake does not publish an owner's real name, avatar, associated mailbox or enabled/paid status there. Optional human preview uses existing contact-granted profile field intersection only; sending mail to a public handle creates no profile capability. The SMTP acceptance/refusal behavior inherently reveals some service availability despite generic errors; do not claim recipient-existence privacy that SMTP behavior cannot provide.

## Rules, retention and optional AI

Extend the existing portable rule evaluator with a small typed vocabulary: trusted receive time, bridge source, normalized automation category, classification/score provenance, sender-address assertion, attachment presence, priority and age. Keep original metadata immutable and distinguish it from owner-corrected presentation. Match rules against a documented snapshot; do not let one automatic rule silently escalate another. Owner actions may choose collection, tags, priority, quarantine, notification suppression, archive deadline or local erase deadline. AI returns labels/scores only and cannot supply executable conditions, arbitrary URLs, RPC calls or policy changes. Sender instructions inside a message are data, including requests to disable filters or grant access.

Propose a visible default: suspected spam stays quiet in quarantine for 30 days, then enters a seven-day recoverable trash period before owner-local erasure. The owner must see and approve this retention when enabling the feature, can change it, and can pin/reclassify an item to cancel the timer. This is a proposed product default, not an existing rule or an assertion that a classifier cannot make mistakes. Ordinary receipts and invitations have no automatic destructive default; owners may choose age-based archive or erase rules. AI-driven destructive rules require an explicit owner rule selecting that classification condition; the model itself never authorizes deletion.

Use provider acceptance time for age, never a sender's `Date`. Duplicates and classifier retries do not reset age. Record the matching rule revision, evidence/provenance, calculated deadline and item revision; when work executes, recheck live owner policy, pin/hold/correction status, membership of the target collection and current revision. A reclassification or rule change cancels/recomputes old work explicitly. No timer from an old restored snapshot may override a newer known erase/policy frontier; an offline recovery cannot know later changes absent from its declared checkpoint. Keep a small local audit of why a timer acted without retaining supposedly erased body content in logs.

“Archive” means removing an item from the active inbox while retaining it; “cold archive” means a storage placement; “erase” removes this owner's retained content under the checkpoint/GC policy. They are separate actions. Sender tombstones, other participants' copies and private recipient retention remain governed by the conversation contract. Schedule bounded batches using the owner's earliest deadline and durable existing alarm/work infrastructure; no actor or timer per email, no per-item globally hot scheduler, and no unbounded full-account scan each minute.

`@cf/cloudflare/clef-flash` is currently documented as a 9B decision model with a 24,576-token context. It accepts state plus typed questions and returns probabilities rather than a free-form mail assistant response. Listed cost is $0.038 per million input tokens, with no output-token charge. Its availability and low input price make it a candidate classifier, not evidence of spam accuracy. Use bounded text-only state and fixed typed questions for independent labels such as spam, conversation/outreach, promotion, verification/code and automation, with urgency separate. Do not reduce the task to binary spam detection or treat mutually overlapping categories as one exclusive choice. Validate result shape/ranges and pin the model/schema version. [Cloudflare model documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/clef-flash/)

Benchmark against owner-relevant multilingual messages, real newsletters, authentication codes, personal replies, forged headers and injected instructions before selecting thresholds. “Suspected spam” must remain a revisable label. Evaluate false positives separately from overall accuracy. On timeout, malformed output, exhausted budget or model change, preserve the message and apply deterministic safe defaults; no automatic retry storm or model-based increase in sender trust. Do not train a new reputation graph by publishing private email associations.

## Cost and abuse budgets

Cloudflare lists inbound routing count as unlimited, but charges Worker processing. Arbitrary-recipient sending requires Workers Paid; 3,000 outbound messages are included per provider account/month, then $0.35 per thousand. Accepted sends, including hard bounces, count; this is not an allowance for every Bindle user. [Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/platform/pricing/)

At the listed model rate, 2,000 input tokens cost about $0.000076. Ten thousand such classifications cost $0.76, already more than the $0.4167 monthly gross equivalent of a $5/year account, before storage, mail, CPU or other features. A current paid entitlement therefore cannot imply unlimited AI mail processing. Start with a small owner allowance—provisionally 100,000 input tokens/month, about $0.0038 at today's rate—and a separate service-wide spend ceiling; refine after measurements. That may classify only roughly fifty 2,000-token messages, so settings must explain heuristic fallback rather than silently calling the service broken.

Give the bridge an owner-selected finite subbudget within covered account work/storage/AI allowances. Receipt counters are additional to existing generic request/covered-work/storage-write guards, not a replacement. Aggregate owner counters survive alias changes, token refresh, bridge re-enable and live-window restoration; classification budget changes do not increase receipt capacity. Recheck current paid entitlement and exact ingress opt-in before classifier enqueue/invocation; disabling classification cancels queued inference without disabling otherwise eligible receipt intake. Public-address spam must not silently spend the full allowance for ordinary applications or admitted conversations. Preserve bounded read/export and protective block/disable/erase capacity when bridge budgets are exhausted.

Reuse the current injected combined activity/storage-write budget and sharded lease approach as a pattern, not the unused legacy monthly `AccountWorkBudget` class or the embedding-specific tariff. Finite prefunded leases must be deducted from a durable cap before allocation, bound to shard/epoch, non-overlapping and unreusable after a crash; fail closed on renewal uncertainty. Account for the maximum outstanding leased allowance when defining the hard limit. Reconciliation never refunds consumed covered-work debits, prefunded service leases or outcome-unknown provider sends. Only provably unused storage/token reservations may be released under the same reservation identity; a crash or missing usage response is not proof of unused capacity. A few infrequent budget coordinators are acceptable; every email must not transact against one globally hot database. Domain and sender keys need bounded cardinality/TTL so randomized senders cannot allocate unlimited counters. Salted/HMAC abuse identifiers still need retention and access controls.

Publish conservative concrete byte, event and response ceilings before launch, based on actual plan economics and load tests, rather than inventing a guarantee from platform “unlimited” routing. Authenticated spam can still spend money up to the chosen caps. Circuit breakers should pause AI first and optional replies next; storage/ingress rejection protects the remaining hard budget. Security/setup mail must not be starved by public intake. Observe aggregate cost, false-positive correction rate, acceptance latency, backlog, suppressed replies and durable failure outcomes without logging bodies, codes, capabilities or complete private address lists.

## Optional coalesced limit alerts

Reuse the shared [rejection-alert design](conversation-contact-design.md#coalesced-rejection-alerts): an explicit owner account-summary event, not a fake unread message. Proposed push/email toggles default off. A verified active ingress may contribute bounded reason counts to one owner summary, with saturation/sampling under load rather than a durable write or notification for every refusal. Unknown recipients, forged links and suspended/deleted accounts do not trigger owner interaction. Disabled or lapsed email intake sends neither bridge prompts nor limit alerts; existing records remain available under normal account rules.

Propose at most one push summary per owner/hour and one email digest/day, with fixed verified owner destinations, capped device fanout, quiet hours, master consent and global notification/mail budget. Pending summary sends recheck account/source incarnation and current consent; global pause or exhaustion may suppress them. A temporary classifier skip is a separate owner status from an actual refused delivery; quarantined accepted content must not be counted as rejected. Keep details in the authenticated account view and include no original sender, subject, capability or attacker-chosen URL in alerts. No alert email goes to incoming `From`/`Reply-To`; no sender acknowledgment is generated merely because the owner hit a limit. Preserve security-mail reserves and bounded protective controls; do not retry an uncertain send into an alert storm.

## Implementation phases and decision gates

1. **Specify and test admission without deployment.** Finalize provenance/claim schemas, settings, current-paid checks, MIME constraints, exact deduplication and safe rules. Exercise worker/node injected adapters and public conversation contract boundaries. Add malicious MIME, forged authentication results, address-role mismatches, owner/link window races, parent-token rotation, all-alias aggregation, idempotent retries, per-ingress classification opt-in and independent budget exhaustion. Add bounded limit-alert storm, account suspension and queued-consent-change tests. Keep all receiving/DNS changes disabled.
2. **Durable owner-only intake.** Add the inbound adapter behind configuration, receipts and pending invitations, encrypted owner-bucket checkpoint integration and consistent backup. Crash-test every reserve/blob/commit/ACK boundary, downgrade races, retention restoration, account removal and no globally hot per-message state. Initially disable AI and automatic replies.
3. **Verified continuation and bounded notices.** Verify actual domain/sender configuration and transport rejection behavior in an authorized staging setup. Test mail scanners, forwarded invitation links, wrong-account association, new-account recovery completion, external-provider handoff, owner accept-before-verification, suppression and lost send responses. Browser tests must demonstrate that GET/login/signup never auto-admits or sends participant content.
4. **Optional classification and retention.** Run fixed-schema Clef evaluations and cost/load tests, expose clear defaults and correction controls, then enable only with agreed finite budgets. Validate destructive rule revocation, false-positive recovery and notification rechecks.
5. **Production gate.** Require the repository's existing security/release gates plus real authorized DNS/routing/sending evidence, provider/header trust-boundary evidence, deliverability and bounce/suppression tests, independent security review and human sign-off. Configuration presence is not production verification.

Unresolved product choices are the precise currently-paid eligibility confirmation rule, published quotas, maximum MIME/attachment sizes, default retention approval, re-enable behavior after paid lapse, and whether strict immediate-reply support justifies a root-domain sender identity. The recommended fallback remains safe intake with no unsafe automated reply. Protocol choices still needing exact schemas are continuation proof freshness, cross-provider mailbox-proof trust, canonical ingress fingerprints, public email-provenance capability discovery and crash-consistent notice claims. None may silently relax existing identity/admission requirements.
