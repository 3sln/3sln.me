## Current independent review — signed peers and local approval

The preferred model is coherent without a permanent initiating administrator. The initiating signed document identifies the conversation and proposes its initial state, including the shared secret and candidate peer identities. Each owner's accepted-peer set is authoritative only for that owner's local behavior. Alice approving Bob does not make Bob approve Alice and does not let either approve Carol for the other. The genesis signature establishes provenance, not everybody's consent, universal membership or privileged ownership of everybody's copies.

The secret is an additional possession gate. Every visible contribution still needs an independently bound author signature and receiver-local approval of that author. The signing proof binds immutable event content and conversation identity; a MAC under the shared secret cannot establish which holder authored it. Receiving the secret before local approval is allowed in this model, unlike the earlier participant-token proposal, because the secret alone must confer no right to visible messages, history, enrollment, owner actions or unbounded storage.

| Option | Benefit | Limitation and review conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared secret + identity signatures + local peer approval | Small common bootstrap, no permanently privileged creator, every owner controls accepted peers. Ordinary writes work without a central issuer/sequencer. | Secret leakage weakens the possession gate; local identity/approval checks are essential. Rotation converges asynchronously. Recommended for the user's chosen semantics if these limits remain explicit. |
| Initiator-issued per-party tokens | Explicit named scopes, expiry and separate issuance; signed tokens can be verified offline. | Adds initiator issuance/refresh and governance/key dependency unless delegated. It does not eliminate each receiver's consent checks or partitioned revocation. Do not claim the issuer must be online for every send. Suitable when the product actually wants an issuer's membership policy. |
| Recipient-issued per-peer conversation grants | Each recipient can revoke delivery authority independently; a leaked grant has a narrower audience. | Up to quadratic directed grant relationships and renewal/discovery work for a full group. Reasonable for bounded groups or a future stricter delivery profile; not necessary to impose a central administrator. |
| Identity signatures and local approvals without a common secret | Least shared-secret distribution/rotation work; authority remains explicit. | Loses the extra possession barrier against outsiders approaching a known conversation. Requires strong admission and abuse limits. A viable alternate profile, not an automatic downgrade. |

Independent requirements for the preferred profile:

1. **Directed approval and provenance.** Separate candidate peer identities advertised by signed state from each owner's live approved incoming/outgoing peers. A remote change is a proposal; it cannot mutate another owner's accepted list. Independently validate/pin hosted identity keys so a genesis author cannot rename their own key as another provider's account.
2. **Bounded unknown peers.** A valid secret or signature from an unapproved actor may produce only a bounded pending introduction, restricted quarantine or rejection according to owner policy. It cannot force body/archive storage, previews, notifications or unlimited signature/discovery work. Automatic requests and repeated proposals consume budgets before expensive warm/fetch work.
3. **Local audience and complete obligations.** Each sender signs an immutable audience snapshot derived from their own accepted eligible peers. Durable local acceptance commits event, idempotency and the whole snapshot outbox. Each receiver separately checks its approval of the original actor, local block, history entitlement and admission incarnation. The sender cannot promise universal delivery to a global membership that does not exist.
4. **Order and conflicts.** Signed immutable events, per-author serialized streams and explicit causal parents replace a global sequence. Deterministic display ties are not real chronological order or evidence of completeness. Conflicting same-author revisions are visible equivocation/conflicts; never silently pick a signature variant or discard retained originals. Fingerprints use canonical payload bytes, not randomized signature bytes.
5. **Local revocation.** Reject new acquisitions from an actor as soon as that receiver removes/blocks them, regardless of shared-secret possession. Remote peers may continue under stale approval state during a partition. Claimed timestamps cannot prove a delayed event predates removal. Reacceptance creates a new local admission incarnation and does not backfill history automatically.
6. **Secret rotation.** No universally current epoch is implied. Each recipient explicitly accepts allowed epoch proposals and defines any bounded overlap. New independent secrets travel only over authenticated locally admitted channels; old-secret possession alone cannot obtain a replacement. Content event identity stays stable across a transport-secret retry; its per-recipient delivery proof binds recipient, admission incarnation and accepted epoch. Approved peers can still leak the replacement. Blocking does not wait for global rotation.
7. **Receipt authority.** Signed recipient/provider ACKs bind conversation, immutable event digest/ID, receiving owner and local admission incarnation. One peer cannot acknowledge everybody's storage. Complete delivery concerns only the original sender snapshot; a changed local peer list cannot shrink its denominator. A durable-delivery assertion is neither human reading nor independently audited provider storage.
8. **Content and history.** Edits, sender-deletion markers, reactions and transparent revision peeks retain the earlier owner-copy rules. New local approval is permission for future exchange, not old-history access. Later edits/reactions cannot leak unseen parent bodies. Rejected/canceled body delivery yields no new peek grant, and local erasure survives delayed retries and restore.
9. **Untrusted synchronization.** Never infer approval from an event's recipients or fetch arbitrary dependency URLs. Bounded missing-parent/reconciliation requests use pinned, authorized peer endpoints. A peer can withhold events or acknowledgments; signatures detect alteration/equivocation, not completeness or honesty.
10. **Private settings and signals.** Local peer approval need not expose the complete private approval graph. Rejection/status responses should disclose only necessary outcomes. Default-off prospective read receipts and short-lived presence use approved audiences and immutable event IDs/per-author ranges. No global read cursor, per-heartbeat archive write or restored historical presence.
11. **Local storage and public contract.** Each owner resource implements the URI-identified conversation interface, including local approval, incoming signed-event exchange and receipt semantics. DO/account/per-conversation placement is a provider choice. Local snapshot/journal fencing, independent retention and crash-safe outboxes remain necessary; no shared R2 bucket or central actor is a protocol requirement.
12. **Version boundary.** Existing v1 bearer-only unverified contributions are not automatically accepted as the new signed-identity profile. Negotiate an explicit version/capability change, pin identity provenance, reestablish local approvals and preserve historical proof labels. There is no silent “signature optional” fallback.

### Public identity-key discovery

Current provider discovery is host-level `/.well-known/bindle/manifest.json`, not a user manifest. The [protocol](../spec/protocol.md) explicitly rejects `https://user@host/...` as account routing: that syntax is URL userinfo. The implemented public conversation identity route is `/api/conversations/identity/<UUID>`. Its [HTTP guard](../../packages/conversations/http.mjs) requires a valid UUID and active account before calling the account adapter; [runtime output](../../packages/conversations/runtime.mjs) contains only origin, owner and explicitly constructed public P-256 coordinates. It does not return the private JWK field `d`.

Two limits matter: the route is outside the general `FEDERATION_ENABLED` routing block and does not have a separate owner public-profile-consent gate; and its `conversationIdentity` → `signingKey` path may create a signing key for an active account that lacks one. Unknown/inactive IDs are rejected first. This is an existing conversation-specific public identity endpoint, not an implemented generic Bindle user manifest or evidence of a fully read-only discovery path.

The proposed generic identity descriptor should be a minimal stable-subject HTTPS document resolved through the user-requested host-manifest `directory_url`, using `GET ?handle=<URI-encoded full handle>`. The directory resolves the public handle to the stable UUID identity document; it is not an arbitrary host resolver. Require:

- Stable identity and issuer/origin binding distinct from mutable handle aliases; bounded read-only lookup with no account/key creation on public GET. Missing/inactive/unpublished keys fail explicitly. Do not follow arbitrary key URLs, redirects or query-supplied identity authorities.
- Public keys only, explicit key ID, algorithm, purpose and validity/status. Conversation signing, encryption/key agreement, OAuth issuer keys and passkey authentication credentials have different roles. Public discovery must not imply their interchangeability or publish private recovery material.
- Pin identity/key provenance on admission. A replaced key in a fresh manifest cannot silently rebind an accepted participant. Define signed continuity, recovery/compromise handling and downgrade/fork behavior; uncertain changes require local review and fail closed for new authorization.
- Retain historical public verification material and the pinned context needed to verify retained signed events. A currently revoked key need not erase old evidence; signatures from a compromised key may remain cryptographically valid without establishing trustworthy authorship/time. No timestamp-only claim of safety before compromise.
- Identity document and directory expose only required public verification/protocol/routing metadata: no real/display name, avatar, bio, email or implicit profile expansion. Optional presentation comes from the separately authorized contact profile below. The host manifest describes the provider without looking up a person. The separately requested directory necessarily discloses existence of deliberately public handles; acknowledge this and restrict it to published numeric/paid aliases, never associated private login/recovery/notice emails.
- Exactly one bounded `handle` query, parsed/decoded once. Reject duplicates, malformed encodings, unexpected parameters, control characters, embedded URL credentials/path syntax and foreign-provider handles. Normalize only the documented provider/IDNA alias rules, not arbitrary email case folding or repeated percent decoding.
- Validate/pin the manifest's directory to the issuer origin or an explicitly approved delegation; omit ambient credentials, reject unsafe redirects and apply network/response/time limits. A caller cannot make this directory fetch arbitrary remote hosts or follow a user-supplied identity URL.
- Resolve only established active public assignments to a minimal canonical identity reference, with appropriate unknown/unpublished 404 behavior, finite caching and abuse limits. Alias changes/reassignment must not transfer an existing pinned identity's trust to a new UUID. No account creation, key generation or private-history warming on either directory or identity GET.

### Optional contact-scoped profile — latest privacy follow-up

Keep identity verification usable without a profile. A display name/avatar is optional self-asserted presentation, not verified identity, local peer approval, contact authority or history entitlement. Refusal to share, a missing field, disabled profile sharing or an expired/revoked contact capability must not invalidate a still-valid pinned identity or silently fall back to unscoped profile disclosure.

The existing [contact profile implementation](../../packages/beaming/contacts.mjs) already supplies the narrow feature needed. A signed contact endpoint GET negotiating `application/vnd.bindle.contact-profile+json` reaches `contactProfile`/`publicProfile`. It inspects the exact live endpoint, signing key, grant and expiry, rejects quarantine, then intersects the owner's global `discloseToContacts` switch with that particular grant's `profileFields` allowlist. Defaults are false and empty; selected profile is `null` when sharing is disabled or no fields are allowed. File-offer permission is independent. A different link for the same person must not widen the fields available through this one.

Reuse this capability-scoped representation and the existing owner controls; do not add a public identity-profile framework. Existing generic profile resource reads use their own app/OAuth grant semantics and cannot be substituted for this contact lookup without preserving its exact live field intersection. Optional profile data must not be copied into `identity.json`, unscoped directory output, key lookup results, notifications or public caches.

Avatar URLs remain hostile metadata. The [existing contact UI](../../apps/web/src/ui/release-beaming.mjs) presents an explicit external avatar link with `noreferrer noopener`; it does not automatically request the image. Current HTTPS/no-userinfo validation is not proof against tracking, redirects, private-address fetches, malicious media or provider-side SSRF. Preserve the no-auto-fetch baseline. Any later rendering/proxy path needs explicit origin/redirect/address/content bounds, isolated media handling and no contact signature, token, cookies or referrer leakage. Render names/bios as text.

Required narrow verification includes global sharing false, empty/exact field sets, two differently scoped links to one identity, changed field permissions, expired/revoked/retired-key/quarantined links, no scope union in caches, absent profile with valid identity, safe text rendering and no remote avatar request during lookup/review. Capability profile responses remain private/no-store; revocation stops future authorized reads but cannot erase values already deliberately disclosed.

The stable proposal's later privacy update was reviewed on 2026-10-13. Its identity/directory fields are now explicitly minimal; optional presentation reuses the actual contact capability and exact field intersection, while assigned app profile access retains its separate authorization. It preserves the no-auto-fetch avatar baseline, specifies no broader fallback on refusal/expiry/revocation, and correctly notes that the existing contact response's handle-derived `identityURI` is not itself the stable cryptographic identity binding. This narrow follow-up addresses the latest privacy requirement at design level; no new public-profile framework or runtime behavior is claimed.

The stable 2026-10-13 rewrite was reviewed separately against the latest requirements above, including the subsequently added `directory_url`, return-control bootstrap and minimal identity/optional contact-profile privacy requirements. It now describes the user's preferred shared-secret, independently signed, locally approved model without retaining a hidden central sequencer or permanent genesis administrator. ## Existing behavior and evidence boundary

- [Conversation service](../../packages/conversations/index.mjs) uses a shared epoch secret, signed membership and provider-held identity keys. Pending invitations disclose that shared secret before admission. This is different from the requested independent participant credentials.
- [Runtime integration](../../packages/conversations/runtime.mjs) and [Worker adapter](../../apps/worker/objects.mjs) place conversation coordination and recipient state within account actors. Local inbound events create owner inbox copies with separate deduplication receipts; this does not establish independent-provider conformance.
- [Current shelving](../../packages/runtime/accounts.mjs) moves bounded message/resource metadata to an account archive. Conversation membership, history and outboxes are not included in that archival profile. Existing warm/shelf behavior must not be cited as a completed conversation archive implementation.
- [Public contact UI](../../apps/web/src/ui/release-public.mjs) is a standalone message composer. [Contact endpoints](../spec/contact-tokens.md) are signed query capabilities with live scope, quotas and revocation. A contact URL grants neither sender identity nor sign-in.
- [Signup flows](../../apps/web/src/bl/release.mjs) retain contact verification, primary credential and tested recovery requirements. A completed signup or login is not consent to send, subscribe, accept membership or disclose a contact capability to an external app.
- The [current manifest](../../apps/web/manifest.webmanifest) has no protocol handler. No browser, OS handoff, operational cost or provider-delivery claim was tested by this document review.

## Initial actor partition and storage judgment — superseded

A hybrid target is reasonable: a conversation actor owns ordering, membership, submission authority and durable fanout; each account actor owns its independently stored recipient copy, local admissions, storage quota, search and notification decisions. A conversation actor cannot force a recipient to retain data. This separates busy conversations from unrelated account operations and makes the ordering boundary explicit. It adds actor calls, durable cross-actor outboxes and reconciliation; acceptance cannot be a transaction across all recipients.

Keeping conversation coordinators inside an account actor is a viable bounded transition and may reduce calls for small local traffic. Its cost is head-of-line blocking and shared failure/recovery scope across that user's conversations and other account operations. Per-conversation actors still serialize one hot conversation, so they do not remove the need for size/rate/queue limits. Compare cold-start frequency, mutation rate, fanout, object-storage operations, durable database size, active duration and contention under representative workloads before selecting a crossover.

Actor count alone is not a measure of running duration cost. Cloudflare documents hibernation/inactivity separately from running objects; pending operations can retain in-memory activity. It also explicitly provides no reliable shutdown hook. Therefore dirty accepted state must be incrementally durable, and archival must be an explicit recoverable operation. [Cloudflare Durable Object lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/)

Required warm/shelf invariants:

1. A minimal durable head records owner/conversation, schema version, generation, committed archive manifest/digest and the durable journal boundary. Authenticating and routing a request must not require trusting a caller-selected archive key.
2. Acceptance persists mutation, idempotency and outbox obligations before acknowledgment. In-memory state is always disposable. Reads and mutations resolve against the committed head plus all later durable journal entries.
3. A shelf operation captures a specific revision. Write immutable chunks and a manifest; verify size/digest/identity; atomically commit the new head only if generation and captured revision still match. If mutations continue during upload, preserve their journal tail or restart compaction.
4. R2 upload, head commit and DO cleanup are separate failure boundaries. Failed/ambiguous upload or conditional conflict leaves the old head and durable journal usable. A new head cannot reference missing chunks. Cleanup may delete only rows demonstrably represented by that committed head, never a newer write.
5. Restore validates the entire manifest and applies bounded pages idempotently; do not merge stale membership, active tokens, revocation state, tombstones or acknowledgments over a newer durable head. Partial restoration is not an active conversation.
6. Queue/notification/search retries and revocation remain recoverable through shelving. A conversation with pending work can be cold only if a durable scheduler and required outboxes remain available independently; archiving them without a wake path loses accepted work.
7. Archive retention/garbage collection must fence concurrent readers, in-flight warm operations and restore. Keep referenced generations until safe; bound abandoned snapshots and unreferenced chunks. Account deletion needs separate key/object cleanup and must not resurrect from an archive.
8. Use bounded immutable segments/checkpoints rather than rewriting entire history per message. Whole-history warming also makes an old busy thread an avoidable latency and memory failure boundary.

R2 offers strongly consistent object operations and conditional writes, including ETag conditions; these do not create a transaction spanning R2 and actor storage. A failed conditional write must be handled as a conflict, not success. The application's durable head and recovery protocol remain necessary. [R2 consistency](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/), [R2 Workers API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/workers/workers-api-reference/)

## Edits, sender deletion, reactions and optional activity

The user requested transparent edits, sender-deletion markers with peekable already-received content, reactions, optional typing/activity/online/status, and optional read receipts. Review these as distinct protocols with separate authorization and privacy rules.

| Feature | Required semantics and adversarial cases |
| --- | --- |
| Edit history | Preserve original and ordered revisions with author, acceptance sequence and time; enforce author-specific edit authority and expected content revision. A stale edit must conflict rather than overwrite an intervening edit. Display that a message changed; viewing history must not fetch unauthorized old content. |
| Sender deletion | Append a visible sender-deleted marker without erasing independent recipient copies. Already-received original/revisions remain peekable by that owner according to their retention choices. State this behavior before sending/deleting; do not label it universal erasure. Administrative moderation, if introduced, needs a separate visibly attributed event and policy. |
| Owner erasure | Recipient can erase their own local content. Preserve the smallest required tombstone/dedup record to prevent replay from restoring it; body/history/blob/search cleanup must honor that choice. Sender changes cannot undo it. Exported copies and other owners' retention remain independent. |
| Visibility after membership changes | A mutation's audience cannot be only the current membership. Intersect the relevant original-message visibility with current delivery/read policy. A new member must not receive old bodies in edits, reaction snippets or deletion previews; a removed member must not receive future mutations unless a separately approved continuity rule allows them. History sharing needs explicit consent. |
| Deletion before initial delivery acknowledgment | A lost acknowledgment does not prove absence of an owner copy. Deliver/reconcile causally using stable message and mutation IDs. Specify whether an unaccepted original is canceled or delivered with its marker. Do not expose peekable content to someone who never received it through an authorized path. Subsequent body retries cannot overwrite a deletion marker or restore locally erased content. |
| Reactions | Bind authenticated participant, original message, normalized bounded reaction, operation ID and event sequence. Define add/remove/set and duplicate retry behavior; bound per-message/per-participant totals and accepted emoji/labels. Reaction removal must not remove another participant's reaction. Quota and notification policy must limit reaction spam. |
| Local read state versus shared receipt | Reading locally updates owner state independently. Provider delivery acknowledgment is not reading, and notification display is not reading. Shared receipts require opt-in plus scoped audience; disabling sharing blocks future emission. No server-side inference of reading from fetching, previews, search or delivery. |
| Receipt privacy | Enabling sharing must not retroactively reveal old reads or publish an existing maximum-read watermark by default. Define how multiple devices and independently stored copies reconcile newly shared reads without filling hidden gaps. Disabling cannot retract already disclosed receipts, but durable local receipt queues must obey the latest opt-out before sending. |
| Typing/activity/online/status | Default off; separately selectable categories, explicit audience and block override. Typing/online signals use short TTL and coarse rate limits; dropped/expired events disappear without durable replay. Explicit status may be longer-lived but needs owner-selected visibility and expiry. Do not derive or disclose activity from ordinary API traffic without consent. |
| Presence abuse and cost | Debounce and coalesce; bound subscriptions and fanout. Avoid per-keystroke R2 writes, long-lived actor timers or waking cold history for ephemeral status. Offline peers cannot receive a replay that makes stale typing appear current. Blocking/permission changes invalidate subscriptions promptly. |

Sender deletion and removal are not cryptographic recall. The provider custody model remains unchanged: authorized recipients can retain their independent copy, and providers handling plaintext can read it. Peek UI must use the existing inert renderer and must not erase evidence of the sender's deletion marker when expanded.

## Public contract and independent implementations

All requested features need a portable public conversation contract, including edits, sender deletion/history, reactions, optional presence/status and read receipts. UI-specific routes cannot be their sole specification. The [existing v1 artifact](../artefacts/conversation-v1.json) is a provisional local submission-envelope schema using shared-secret epochs; it is not a full generic resource interface and must not be silently relabeled with participant-token semantics.

The successor needs its own immutable identifying contract URI, versioned method/argument/result/error definitions, proof/canonicalization profile, event types and capability negotiation. Match the repository's formal contract publication layout rather than assuming the old artifact's filename defines the future namespace. Publishing an artifact and advertising runtime support are separate changes; this review authorizes neither publication nor conformance claims.

Required contract checks:

- Separate the canonical conversation authority URI from the owner-local resource ID for an independent copy. Local resource IDs address the current owner's scope; they cannot expose another owner's copy or implicitly grant authority-side control. Provider migration needs an explicit identity/continuity policy.
- Reuse the existing RPC resource/contract/method envelope and current account/app grant intersection where applicable. A generic app's resource token and a participant's submission proof have different authority. Neither may expand the other's permissions.
- Define the portable peer exchange for admission, events, receipts and synchronization alongside owner operations. Node, Worker and other implementations must share wire semantics without Cloudflare actor IDs, R2 keys or internal queue identifiers in the public contract.
- Advertise exact implemented optional features and limits. Unknown methods, event versions or required capability values fail explicitly; optional extensions cannot weaken authentication, downgrade privacy or silently enable sharing.
- Freeze the URI/version/content digest used for consent. New methods or broader grants need explicit new authorization. Unsupported external providers remain visibly unsupported, without fallback into a private transport that appears conformant.
- Provide conformance vectors for signed events, participant tokens, stale revisions, receipt errors, ordered edit/deletion/reaction behavior and presence/receipt privacy before claiming portability. Two copies of one implementation are insufficient independent-provider evidence.

| Direct Bindle URI | Native handler launch opens review only. Unknown handler, chooser dismissal and unsuitable app are recoverable. The URI must not contain message contents or reusable secrets. |
| Installed PWA | An optional registered handler opens a safe HTTPS handler route. Multiple installs, denied registration, browser-only opening and unsupported browsers all work through the ordinary website. Handler registration/launch is not app authentication. |

WHATWG permits safelisted schemes or `web+` followed by ASCII letters for `registerProtocolHandler`; `bindle` is not safelisted. The registered handler is same-origin HTTP(S), uses `%s`, and receives an encoded serialization of the input URI. Thus `web+bindle` can be proposed for web handling; ordinary `bindle:` requires separate native/platform handling and must not be promised as a universal PWA feature. Confidential data should not be embedded in these URLs. [WHATWG custom handlers](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/system-state.html#custom-handlers)

Chrome's manifest feature uses `protocol_handlers` with `protocol` and a handler `url` containing `%s`. Installation/launch are user-agent and platform behaviors; documentation does not demonstrate this project's actual cross-browser or OS support. Keep HTTPS entry and user-visible fallback, and verify the target browser/OS matrix before shipping the enhancement. [Chrome protocol-handler documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/best-practices/url-protocol-handler)

## Earlier federation-first replacement — admin chain now superseded

The late user direction changes the coordination model, not merely actor placement. A centralized sequencer can receive requests from many providers; it is not inherently same-provider-only. Its separate weakness is that everybody depends on that sequencer for new canonical acceptance. Independently writable replicas remove that dependency by accepting different facts locally and reconciling signed immutable events later. The earlier review's canonical sequence, one global membership cutover and central acceptance journal must not carry over as unchanged guarantees.

### Independent recommendation and tradeoffs

Use one public conversation resource per participant's owner copy, a stable conversation identity independent of any mandatory live host, signed immutable author events and direct sender fanout. Every provider independently controls its local journal, storage, delivery admissions and owner authorization. Serialize each author's stream within their own provider; retain causal parent references for replies, edits and observed events. A deterministic display tie-break can make replicas present the same known event set consistently. It cannot prove real chronological order, hide missing events, or make a local acceptance globally final.

Keep the policy mechanism narrow: one pinned administrative signing identity initially, a signed predecessor-linked policy chain cached by participants, and separately signed recipient admission. The policy signer is a control-plane authority, not an online message sequencer. Its unavailability prevents roster/permission changes requiring that signature; it need not prevent already admitted peers from exchanging messages under their known policy. Concurrent successor policies are a visible fork requiring an authorized resolution, not a reason to merge permissions or choose a winner by timestamp/hash. Multiple administrative writers or strict immediate global revocation would require an additional reviewed protocol; neither is implicit here.

| Boundary | Required replacement guarantee |
| --- | --- |
| Participant identity | Event signatures bind the conversation incarnation, stable event ID, author identity/key generation, author stream position/predecessor, causal dependencies, policy digest and immutable payload digest. Display names and bearer possession do not replace author proof. Detect conflicting signed events at the same author position. |
| Local resource grants | Each owner's app token authorizes only that owner's selected public resource/methods. Peer delivery authority is separately recipient-issued/admitted and scoped to conversation, intended actor/provider and admission incarnation. No one transferable group secret grants everybody's inbox access. |
| Signed policy and admission | Pin the initial policy key through authenticated introduction. Validate its chain and each recipient's consent independently. An admin signature cannot waive recipient quota, blocked status or collection ownership. Key migration needs signed continuity; a fetched new key is not automatically trusted. |
| Concurrent invite/remove/permission changes | Ordinary writers use a specific signed policy digest, never an editable recipient list. Forked successors freeze affected security-sensitive application until valid resolution; retain already received evidence. No permission union, silent last-writer-wins grant expansion or retroactive deletion of local bytes. |
| Partition and removal | Leave/block/revoke acts immediately at the issuing local provider. A disconnected peer may keep sending or accepting under its last known policy. On learning removal, reject newly acquired events from the removed actor according to explicit policy, including possibly legitimate delayed events. There is no simultaneous global cutoff or reliable clock-based proof that a delayed event was authored before removal. |
| Ordering and completeness | Causal dependencies precede dependents; independent concurrent posts have deterministic presentation only. Local cursors are not shared global event positions. Missing-parent, withheld, forked and unavailable content must be distinguishable from an empty complete history. No unrestricted fetch to resolve an unknown parent. |
| Sender acceptance | Atomically commit sender-owned immutable event, replay receipt and outbox entries for the complete eligible roster snapshot known at acceptance. This proves a local obligation, not remote acceptance or universal compliance by a malicious sender. A recipient checks policy/audience and its own admission; it cannot infer successful fanout merely from receiving an event. |
| Recipient acknowledgment | A recipient/provider-signed ACK binds conversation/incarnation, event ID and digest, receiving owner and admission incarnation. Relays and other members cannot ACK on that owner's behalf. It attests the provider's stated durable acceptance, not an independently audited disk write or human read. |
| Delivery status | All-delivered requires ACKs for the original accepted recipient snapshot. Changes in membership cannot reduce that denominator. Missing/expired grants, offline peers, removal and canceled body transfer remain explicit pending/rejected/canceled outcomes. Different senders can hold different receipt knowledge. |
| Retry and retention | Stable author/event/digest/recipient-admission IDs survive restarts and lost ACKs. Authorize before replay disclosure; changed payload under one ID conflicts. Tombstones, per-author replay fences and declared retry horizons survive compaction. Ancient unverifiable retries require reconciliation rather than creating a new message. |
| Transparent mutations | Edits/deletions reference exact original events and author revision ancestry. Author-provider serialization resolves ordinary same-author races; equivocation preserves conflicting signed revisions visibly. A sender marker does not erase owner copies. Deletion received before body remains a marker, with no new body-fetch grant; owner erase defeats late replay. |
| Historical audiences | Edits, reactions, receipt context and re-share cannot give new members unseen old content. Check target visibility and current known admission independently; stale policy does not override a recipient's newer local block/removal. No global promise that all providers learn the same visibility decision instantly. |
| Social signals | Default-off, prospective receipts and ephemeral typing/presence remain. Receipt ranges refer to visible immutable IDs or author-scoped ranges, not a nonexistent canonical sequence. Audience/revocation checks are local and may lag at disconnected peers; TTL bounds stale signals rather than retracting prior disclosure. |
| Storage and actor choice | R2/DO cold storage operates per local replica or account, with local writer-generation fencing and snapshot-plus-tail recovery. A provider may use per-user actors or `(owner, conversation)` actors. No one actor or bucket becomes a protocol authority, and full local deletion must not resurrect from an old checkpoint. |
| Public interface | Owner resources implement the same URI-identified conversation contract. Append/edit/tombstone/react operate through the writer's resource; peers receive signed immutable events and return their own ACKs through a documented portable exchange. Cursors, role names and errors must not imply global order/central acceptance. No private API-only federation path. |

The most important retained compromise is explicit: continued independent writes during partition and instantly enforced global removal cannot both be promised by this design. A future strict short-lived policy lease could trade availability for a bounded stale-policy window, but that is a separate capability with clock/renewal assumptions. It must not silently reintroduce an online dependency into the default contract.
